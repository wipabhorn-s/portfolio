AumStocks

A back-office stock system for small retail shops: one owner runs several branches from one central catalogue, sells through a barcode POS and adjusts stock by typing Thai into a chatbot on the web or in LINE.

Why we built it

Small shops and minimarts often keep stock in a notebook or a spreadsheet, and the numbers drift as soon as there is more than one branch or more than one person selling. We wanted one place where every change to stock is recorded, wherever it happens: on the web, at the till by scanning a barcode, or from a quick LINE message.

Who it is for

Shop owners who run one or more branches, and the staff who sell and receive stock for them. Owners keep one catalogue for every branch and decide, per person and per shop, what each staff member may do. Staff get a quick way to sell and update stock without learning a complicated system. Admins look after the whole platform.

What it does

  • A central product catalogue: a product is created once and listed in any shop, and each shop keeps its own price, stock and low-stock point.
  • Stock in, adjust and transfer between shops, with the cost of each received lot (the oldest lot is used first) and a stock history that is never edited.
  • A point-of-sale screen: pick products, or scan QR and EAN-13 barcodes with the camera on Plus and Pro. Bills keep the price and cost at the time of sale and can be voided with a reason.
  • A chatbot on the web and in LINE: type เพิ่มโค้ก10 ("add 10 Coke"), an LLM (Ollama) reads it with a rule-based fallback, and the change is saved only after the user confirms.
  • AI restock, clearance and promotion suggestions on Pro, pushed live to everyone viewing that shop.
  • Free, Plus (฿2,499 a year) and Pro (฿3,499 a year) plans paid by card through Stripe. When a paid plan expires, the owner's shops become read-only until renewal.
  • Staff permissions per shop, dashboards, in-app notifications, an admin console with an audit log, and a Thai / English interface.

My part

  • Gathered the requirements and wrote the SRS: five kinds of user, the business rules, the plan limits and 122 functional requirements. Then wrote the team guide (AGENTS.md) that every developer and AI agent followed.
  • Sign-in and accounts: JWT with refresh tokens, 2FA on every sign-in method, email verification, forgotten and reset passwords, Google and LINE login with account linking, and changing the email.
  • Payments: Stripe card payments on our own page, upgrades that charge only the difference, cancelling and retrying pending payments, and payment history.
  • The admin area: managing users and shops, creating admin accounts and an audit log.
  • Team foundations: CI, the shared shadcn theme, the auth and payments database schema, and the switch to Brevo for email.

Requirements and data model

The SRS was the contract between five developers: each person owned a set of modules, and the rules in it, like soft deletes, an append-only stock history and the two permission checks, kept the pieces consistent.

Challenges and decisions

Two checks for every staff action

A staff member's permissions are set per shop, and the owner's plan unlocks features on top of that. I wrote it as one rule in the SRS: every action must pass both the permission the owner gave and the owner's plan. With the rule written down, all five developers checked it the same way.

The server could not send email

The API host blocks outgoing SMTP, so verification and reset emails never left the server. I replaced SMTP with Brevo's HTTP API, which goes out over normal web traffic.

Removed staff could keep working

A JWT is only signed data, so a deleted staff member could keep using the system until their token expired. Now every request checks the account in the database, access tokens last 15 minutes, and sessions continue through refresh tokens.

What I learned

Working in a team of five taught me that the work before the code matters as much as the code. Clear requirements and a shared team guide meant five people, and their AI coding tools, built pieces that fitted together. On the technical side, I learned how much care sign-in and payments need, from removing someone's access immediately to handling upgrades and unfinished payments.
